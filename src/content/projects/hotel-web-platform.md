I built and launched an internal platform that let 8,000-odd hotels create
their own websites. Time to stand up a property site dropped from about six
weeks to two. The company put the annual saving at $2.5M.

I led 10 developers, 2 designers and 2 testers through go-live and the releases
after it.

## The problem

Every property needed a web presence and creating one needed specialists. With
8,000 properties and something like 50,000 potential users across 139
countries, the queue was the cost.

## The thing that made it hard

Two failure modes sat on either side and both would have killed it.

Give property managers too much freedom and you end up with 8,000 off-brand
websites, which is worse than the bottleneck you removed. Give them too little
and they cannot show what makes their hotel worth booking, so they route around
the tool and you have shipped shelfware.

We put the brand constraints into the system rather than into a guidelines PDF.
When the compliant path is also the fastest one, compliance stops being a
review step and starts being the default.

139 countries made all of this sharper. A tool built on one market's
assumptions fails quietly everywhere else. Address formats, image norms, how
much room a translated string needs, what even counts as a hotel in that
market.

## Time to launch

Six weeks down to two, across 8,000 properties, is where the $2.5M comes from.
No licence saving and no headcount cut. The same work moving through the queue
about three times faster.

> adoption number is what turns $2.5M from a projection into a result.

## Getting it used

The 4% lift in retention and conversion came after launch, out of releases that
responded to how people were using it.

Shipping was about halfway. Internal tools get no marketing to make up for a
bad first experience, and a tool nobody opens saves nothing.

I ran workshops with C-suite, engineering and design stakeholders the whole way
through. On a multi-year internal platform, visible progress is what keeps the
funding attached, and I spent more time on that than I expected to.

## Outcomes

- **$2.5M in annual savings**
- Property launches about **3× faster**, from six weeks to two
- **8,000+ hotels** and **50,000+ users** across **139 countries**
- **+4% retention and conversion** from post-launch releases

## What I'd do differently

We should have shipped to a handful of properties much earlier. Designing for
139 countries from the start was right in principle and it meant a long stretch
before real users touched anything. The localization assumptions we got wrong
were the kind a pilot in three markets would have caught in a fortnight.
