I did not set out to become a product manager. I was recommending new issues
and relative value trades across a $750M aerospace and defense portfolio, and
the analytics we had were not good enough.

## The situation

An investment team that needed better portfolio analytics. I volunteered to run
it, which mostly meant I was the person willing to sit with traders and
research analysts and ask what they actually did all day.

## What I owned

Translating stakeholder interviews into user stories and business requirements,
and the launch to **120+** portfolio managers, traders, and research analysts.

## Why it got adopted

I sat next to the users. Every day.

That is not a repeatable methodology, but it explains the outcome better than
anything else. When you use the tool yourself and the people it fails are
people you'll see at lunch, the feedback loop is immediate and the incentive to
build something that actually works is unusually strong. Most internal tools
fail on adoption, and most of that failure is distance between builder and
user.

The domain knowledge mattered too. When a trader described what they needed, I
understood the workflow underneath it, which meant requirements captured intent
rather than a literal transcription of a feature request. Translating
stakeholder language into user stories is mostly an act of interpretation, and
you cannot interpret a domain you don't know.

## The alternate track

The analyst work ran alongside: recommending newly issued securities and
relative value trades, briefing portfolio managers on investor sentiment,
industry headwinds, and market trends.

That training still shows up. Most product arguments come down to a claim about
numbers — whether a metric means what someone says it means, whether a
projection is defensible, whether cost savings are real or accounting. Being
able to check the arithmetic yourself is a durable advantage.

## Outcomes

- Portfolio analytics software launched to **120+** users
- Requirements and user stories translated from stakeholder interviews
- Coverage of a **$750M** aerospace and defense portfolio
- A career change

## What I'd do differently

I would have written down what I was learning about the users. The knowledge
lived in my head because I was embedded with them, which worked for me and
transferred to nobody. The first thing I'd want if I handed that product to
someone else is the thing I never produced.
