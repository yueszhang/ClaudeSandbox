I was product analyst on a digital acceleration program run by a major
technology platform for two apparel retailers. I led six engineers across three
features, built 20 custom audiences, and lifted acquisition and retention KPIs
by around 5%.

The piece I'd point to is the attribution model.

## The attribution work

We built a data-driven attribution model using ML to predict cart abandonment
across the funnel. I wrote the technical design doc.

Attribution matters more than it looks. Last-click systematically overcredits
the bottom of the funnel, so a team optimizing against it keeps tuning the final
step and quietly underinvests everywhere else. Changing the attribution model
changes what every other decision is optimizing toward, which is why it's worth
doing before the conversion work rather than after.

The outcome signal worth citing: **the client's own marketing technology team
extended the model after our engagement ended.** Work that survives the
consultants leaving is a different claim from work that shipped.

## Segmentation

Twenty custom audiences, replacing rule-based segments.

This was before AI was the default answer to everything, and it worked because
both problems are statistical underneath. Segmentation is clustering.
Attribution is credit assignment across a path. Neither needed a transformation
story attached — they needed better models than what was already there.

## What I'd do differently

The modeling and whatever research existed ran as separate tracks. Interviews
fed the backlog, the engineering team fed the models, and the two barely met.
Whatever the interviews contained was almost certainly the hypotheses the
segmentation work should have been testing.
