I ran product for two Fortune 500 retailers in a partner-led acceleration
program. Checkout rate went up 5%. Eight engineers, and somewhere north of 70
user interviews.

## What I owned

Feature prioritization and backlog grooming for the marketing platform, the
research behind it, and the engineering team building it.

## The interviews

The count matters less than what it bought, which was enough conversations to
tell a pattern from a loud complaint.

At low research volumes the roadmap gets set by whoever is most articulate. Get
past about fifty and you can see the frequency distribution instead. The things
suppressing checkout were mostly not what customers volunteered first; they
showed up as hesitation and abandonment rather than as stated complaints, which
meant reading behaviour against what people said.

> **Draft note —** What were the top two or three pain points you found? This
> is where you can show research judgement and right now the page argues for
> the method without reporting a single finding. One specific friction point
> you fixed would carry the whole section.

## Segmentation and attribution

The engineering team put AI into audience segmentation and switched on
data-driven attribution.

This was before AI was the default answer to everything, and it worked because
both problems are statistical underneath. Segmentation is clustering.
Attribution is credit assignment across a path. Neither one needed a
transformation story attached; they needed better models than the rule-based
segments and last-click attribution sitting there.

Attribution mattered more than it looks. Last-click overcredits the bottom of
the funnel, so a team optimising against it keeps tuning the final step and
quietly underinvests everywhere else.

> **Draft note —** How did the segmentation model differ from the rule-based
> segments it replaced, and did it change what the marketing team did day to
> day? An AI project from 2022 that changed someone's behaviour is worth more
> here than the technique.

## Outcomes

- **+5% checkout rate**
- **70+ user interviews** driving prioritization
- AI-based segmentation and data-driven attribution in production
- 8 engineers led through delivery

## What I'd do differently

The research and the modelling ran as separate tracks. Interviews fed the
backlog, the engineering team fed the models, and the two barely met. The
interviews contained the exact hypotheses the segmentation work should have
been testing. Two useful streams that would have been better as one, and I did
not notice until late.
