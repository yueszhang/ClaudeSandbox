I ran an evaluation of six AI tools for Deloitte's Customer Strategy & Design
practice, covering synthetic personas, journey mapping and user research. Two
builds came out of it, avoiding roughly $400K of spend.

Internal work, which made it more honest than most vendor evaluations. Nobody
was being sold to and there was no procurement deadline forcing an answer. The
output either got used by our own practitioners or it sat there.

## What I owned

The evaluation. Eight junior practitioners across three pods, one pod per
category, and the recommendation at the end.

## How it ran

Three pods, six tools, one shared assessment structure so the results could be
compared rather than just collected.

Junior practitioners did the hands-on piloting. That was deliberate. People who
would use these tools daily are better evaluators than a lead reading feature
matrices, and they run into the friction that demos are built to hide.

The decision came after the pilots. For each category we asked whether buying
was the right answer at all, then took that to internal developers and
designers to be argued with rather than settling it in a vendor conversation.

<Diagram name="buildbuy" />

Where each landed:

- **Journey mapping: build.** It is a method the practice is known for, and
  every tool we looked at wanted us to adopt its model of what a journey map
  is. That meant handing over the thing that differentiates us.
- **Synthetic personas: buy.** Fast-moving and model-dependent. Anything we
  built would be behind in two release cycles and a consultancy has no business
  maintaining it.
- **User research: buy.** Real capability, thoroughly commoditized. Building it
  would have been paying to solve a solved problem.

The rule underneath is that you build only where the capability is the
differentiation. Everywhere else you are signing up for maintenance, staffing
and the opportunity cost of engineers who could be on client work.

## Outcomes

- **6 tools** piloted across synthetic personas, journey mapping and user
  research
- **2 builds** shortlisted, avoiding about **$400K**
- **8 practitioners** developed across 3 pods

## What I'd do differently

I set the build-versus-buy criteria after the pilots instead of before. Running
pilots first meant the criteria were shaped by what we had just seen, which is
a quiet way of rationalising a preference you already had. Writing down what
would make a build worth it in advance would have made the recommendation
harder to argue with, including for me.
